# Why full-body movement = joy, and how to use it

Research synthesis behind the jumping-jacks decision. Cited.

## The core findings

**Intensity is a double-edged sword (Dual-Mode Theory, Ekkekakis).** Movement
feels good *below* the ventilatory threshold (labored breathing), gets variable
*around* it, and turns reliably *unpleasant* above it. So: design for **bursts of
hard effort + recovery**, never a sustained grind. Enjoyment is what drives people
to come back, and enjoyment of high-intensity work is *trainable* — it rises over
the first weeks.

**Frame it as dance/play, not a fitness test.** Dance-style full-body movement
produces higher enjoyment *and lower perceived exertion* than intensity-matched
cardio. This is the strongest lever we have: make the big movement expressive and
rhythmic.

**Flow is the design target (Csikszentmihalyi).** Three things we can engineer
directly: a clear goal, immediate unambiguous feedback, and challenge matched to
skill (→ dynamic difficulty). Everything else (time distortion, absorption)
follows.

**Competence + autonomy make it stick (Self-Determination Theory).** In fitness
apps these two predict intrinsic motivation most strongly. Show visible mastery;
give real choices (which special to charge, when to go big, low-impact option).

**Juice every rep (game feel).** "Maximum output for minimum input" — screen
shake, particles, bass, demon ragdoll on each hit. A physical effort that lands
with big feedback *feels worth it*. Exertion-game research (Mueller) agrees:
movement needs its own special feedback; design for the body, not the screen.
Exergames hit ~89% in-activity engagement vs ~62% for plain HIIT.

**Reward variably but ethically.** Combos/streaks/escalating payoffs delight — but
this is the dark-pattern danger zone. Use transparency and *forgiveness* (rest
days, streak protection). Never punish resting an injured body in a health product.

**Honest caveat — don't oversell.** The "expansive posture makes you powerful"
(power-posing) idea does **not** replicate; the original lead author publicly
disavowed it. Build the motivation story on flow, enjoyment, and competence — not
hormones/confidence claims.

## The key detection insight

Webcam jump-detection is genuinely unreliable (vertical motion, blur, leaving
frame) — which is exactly why we cut the old jump. Solution: **detect the jumping-
jack by its POSE SHAPE** (arms overhead + feet apart, transitioning to arms-down +
feet-together), not by the airborne jump. The shape is robust, and it also lets us
ship a **low-impact "step-jack" variant** (step out instead of jump) detected by
the same logic — better accessibility, wider audience.

## Design principles (shortlist)

1. Keep effort in bursts below each player's personal wall.
2. Make the big movement feel like dance/play.
3. Engineer flow: clear goal, instant feedback, skill-matched difficulty.
4. Feed competence relentlessly (visible mastery, unlocks).
5. Give real autonomy (choices + a low-impact option).
6. Juice every rep.
7. Reward variably but honestly (forgiveness, no loss-anxiety).
8. Don't anchor the pitch to debunked science (power posing).

## Recommended mechanic

**Jumping jacks charge an "ultimate" (a screen-clearing Demon Banisher)**, with
occasional music-locked **"frenzy" multiplier events** for peak joy. Self-paced
(autonomy), naturally interval-shaped (safe affect), and a juicy payoff that makes
the exertion feel earned. Detect via the jack *shape*; ship a low-impact variant.
